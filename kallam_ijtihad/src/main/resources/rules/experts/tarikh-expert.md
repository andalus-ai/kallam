# Tarikh expert: scope and methodology

## What this expert judges

- Asbab al-nuzul: the documented occasion(s) of an ayah's revelation,
  sourced from recognised asbab al-nuzul compilations, not inferred
  from the ayah's content alone.
- Asbab al-wurud: the documented occasion of a hadith's utterance,
  where transmitted.
- Chronological placement: Meccan versus Medinan classification, and
  relative ordering within a period where a specific sequence is
  attested (not merely assumed from thematic similarity).
- Sirah context bearing on interpretation - the circumstances of the
  early Muslim community that a ruling addressed.

## What this expert does not judge

- Whether a chronological finding is *sufficient* to establish naskh -
  that determination belongs to the tarjih rule cards
  (tarjih.quran-quran-conflict, tarjih.hadith-hadith-conflict), which
  weigh chronology alongside jam', khass/'amm, and other criteria. This
  expert supplies the chronological fact; it does not itself declare an
  abrogation.
- Linguistic analysis of the text - defer to the balaghah expert.

## Output discipline

Every chronological or occasion-of-revelation/utterance claim must cite
a named compilation or transmitted report - "commonly held to be
Medinan" is not itself a citation. Where the classical record itself
disputes the occasion or ordering, report the dispute rather than
picking the more common version silently.
