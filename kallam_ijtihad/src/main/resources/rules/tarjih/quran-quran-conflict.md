# Tarjih between apparently conflicting ayat

Apply the following in order, stopping at the first step that resolves
the apparent conflict. Most apparent Qur'an-Qur'an conflicts resolve at
step 3 (jam') without needing naskh at all - naskh should be the last
explanation reached for, not the first.

1. **Jam' (reconciliation) - check this first, not last.** Are the ayat
   actually addressing different subjects, different conditions, or
   different circumstances? A large share of apparent contradictions in
   the Qur'an are not contradictions once each ayah's actual scope is
   read precisely. Do not escalate past this step until reconciliation
   has been genuinely attempted, with each ayah's subject, addressee, and
   condition stated explicitly.

2. **Khass over 'amm (specific over general).** If one ayah is general
   and the other specific on the same matter, the specific qualifies
   (yukhassisu) the general. This is takhsis, not naskh - both ayat
   remain in effect, each within its proper scope.

3. **Muqayyad over mutlaq (qualified over unqualified).** Similarly, a
   qualified ruling restricts an unqualified one addressing the same
   subject and legal cause (sabab), rather than replacing it.

4. **Naskh (abrogation) - only with independent chronological
   evidence.** If steps 1-3 do not resolve the conflict, and the
   chronology of revelation is independently established (asbab
   al-nuzul reports, Meccan/Medinan ordering, or an explicit naskh
   report - see the tarikh expert's findings), the later ayah on the
   same specific matter takes legal precedence. Both ayat remain
   recited scripture regardless; naskh affects legal ruling (hukm), not
   textual status.
   - Do not assert naskh from ruling-conflict alone. The chronological
     evidence must be cited and independently checkable, not inferred
     backward from "this ruling seems more recent."
   - Naskh of a Qur'anic ruling by a hadith (rather than by another
     ayah) is itself a disputed usul question across madhahib; treat it
     as requiring explicit justification, not as available by default.

5. If none of the above resolves the conflict, record it as **unresolved
   ikhtilaf** rather than forcing a determination the classical
   methodology itself would not force. This is a legitimate, auditable
   outcome - not a failure of the process.

## Scoring guidance for the expert agent

- +40 if the resolution is grounded in an explicit, cited chronological
  or asbab al-nuzul report, not inferred
- +30 if the khass/'amm or muqayyad/mutlaq relationship is textually
  explicit rather than reconstructed from context
- +20 for each independent classical tafsir source concurring with this
  resolution
- -35 if naskh is asserted without independent chronological citation
- -20 if jam' (step 1) was not genuinely attempted before invoking a
  later step
